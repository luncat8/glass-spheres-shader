# Glass-bubble variants plan v0.3 — hollow see-through wall, GUI slider

## Goal

Replace v0.2 with a single shader that renders up to **32 hollow glass bubbles** with a **real geometric wall** (two refracting surfaces, not a chord-through-solid approximation) and a **GUI slider for wall thickness**. Looks good at modest cost; the slider must move the wall in/out of the bubble center live.

## What's wrong with v0.2

v0.2 (A1/A2/A3) treats each bubble as a solid glass ball: light refracts in at the front surface, marches in a straight line through the interior, and refracts out at the back surface. The "wall" is the geometric chord. That gives tinted glass but **not** the look of an empty bubble whose shell is a thin spherical skin.

## What "real hollow bubble" means

Each bubble is two concentric spheres:
- **Outer surface** at radius `R_out` = sphere radius.
- **Inner surface** at radius `R_in` = `R_out - wallThickness`.

The slider moves `wallThickness` from `0.02 * R_out` (thin shell) to `~0.85 * R_out` (very thick wall, almost a marble). Light:
1. Refracts **into** the glass at the outer surface.
2. Travels a short distance through the wall material.
3. Refracts **out into the air pocket** at the inner surface.
4. Travels a straight line across the air pocket.
5. Refracts **into the glass** at the inner surface on the far side.
6. Refracts **out to the world** at the outer surface on the back.

This is the "shell + cavity + shell" model. Both glass walls produce visible Fresnel rims and reflections. Beer-Lambert tint is applied along the wall chord (geometric, accurate).

## How to get this from one raymarching pass (the cheap simplification)

v0.2's A2/A3 pattern is **raymarch against a SDF, refract on hit, march inside (with negated SDF), refract on second hit**. To get a hollow bubble, the **outer surface is the same sphere SDF**, and the **inner surface is a second sphere SDF at the same center, slightly smaller radius**. We intersect the refracted-in ray against the inner surface using the same analytic ray-sphere math — no inner march needed, the cavity is empty and straight-line.

Why this works for a thin shell:
- The glass wall is geometrically a thin spherical layer. The chord through it is short.
- The path inside the air pocket is just a straight line between the two inner-surface hits.
- Both refractions (outer→in and in→cavity) use the actual sphere normals, so Fresnel and reflection are physically placed.

Why this fails for a **very thick wall** (slider near 0.85 R): the refracted ray inside the glass is still a straight line, but the chord through the wall is now long, so a single `transmit = exp(-absorb * chord)` is correct. No internal march needed. The only failure mode is if the wall is so thick the inner sphere disappears — handled by the slider range cap.

## Single shader, single file: `shaders/XbXXzB_hollow.js`

One shader, designed for **up to 32 bubbles** with **nearest-front Z-sort**, true Fresnel, reflection + refraction, Beer-Lambert through the wall.

### SDF + intersections

Store bubbles as a `vec4[N]` array `Bubbles[i] = vec4(centerX, centerY, centerZ, R_out)`. `N` is fixed at compile time (say `N_MAX = 32`) and only the first `uCount` are active (`uCount` is a uniform; runtime-set via slider, default 8).

Per-bubble `R_in = R_out - uWallThickness`. Animating the centers: time-driven, identical formula to v0.2 A1 (4 base sines, plus per-index phase offsets so up to 32 bubbles are not in lockstep).

For each bubble compute analytically:
- Outer hit: `tE_out, tX_out` from `ray_sphere(ro, rd, center, R_out)`.
- Inner hit (refracted direction needed first, so done per bubble after computing `rd_in`).

### Per-bubble shading (one pass, no chain, no bounce)

```
1.  d = ray_sphere(ro, rd, C, R_out);  if (no hit) skip.
2.  n_out  = normalize(ro + rd*d.tE - C)
3.  F      = fresnel_schlick(dot(-rd, n_out), F0)
4.  rd_in  = refract(rd, n_out, 1.0/IOR)
    if (TIR) treat as full reflection; skip refraction branch
5.  // Now travel through the wall, then exit into the cavity.
    // Analytic: where does rd_in hit the inner sphere of radius R_in?
    d2 = ray_sphere(ro + rd*d.tE + n_out*eps, rd_in, C, R_in)
    if (no inner hit) → degenerate (shell collapsed), use outer-refrac fallback
6.  n_in1  = normalize(ro + rd*d.tE + rd_in*d2.tE - C)   // points outward (toward center->surface)
    rd_cav = refract(rd_in, -n_in1, IOR)                  // glass -> air
    if (TIR) → internal reflection: bounce inside glass (cheap fallback: use reflect(rd_in, -n_in1) as rd_cav and recolor as if bounced)
7.  // Travel straight across the cavity to the other inner surface.
    d3 = ray_sphere(ro + rd*d.tE + rd_in*d2.tE + n_in1*eps, rd_cav, C, R_in)
    if (no inner exit hit) → cavity chord failed; sample env directly
8.  n_in2  = normalize(ro + ... + rd_cav*d3.tE - C)
    rd_out = refract(rd_cav, -n_in2, 1.0/IOR)             // air -> glass
    if (TIR) → bounce inside, fallback similar
9.  // Wall chord: sum of the two glass segments. Approx 2*d2.tE (both walls are
    // near-symmetric for a thin shell). Use the actual sum: d_wall = d2.tE + d4.tE
    (d4 is the second wall chord; compute the same way)
10. transmit = exp(-uAbsorb * d_wall)
11. far_env  = texture(iChannel0, rd_out).rgb
12. inner_env = texture(iChannel1, rd_out).rgb   // alternate env, cheap fake dispersion
13. col_refracted = mix(far_env, inner_env, 0.5) * transmit
14. col_reflected = texture(iChannel0, reflect(rd, n_out)).rgb
15. col = mix(col_refracted, col_reflected, F)
16. // Back-wall inner highlight (Fresnel kick on the far side of the shell, seen
    //    through the cavity) — small additive term that sells the "two surfaces":
    kick = pow(max(dot(-rd_cav, n_in2), 0.0), 8.0) * 0.15
    col += texture(iChannel0, reflect(rd_cav, -n_in2)).rgb * kick
17. // Blend onto background by alpha = (1 - transmit) * something? No — for
    // glass we additive-front-to-back composite (see step 18).
```

### Composite: front-to-back, 4 unrolled sorts

To keep it under 32 spheres with no dynamic indexing on every GLSL driver, do the same fixed-permutation sort pattern from v0.2 A1: 4 outer `if`s pick the **nearest valid** bubble, render it, advance `t = tE + tiny`, then loop. Up to `uCount` iterations, each picking the front-most remaining. With `uCount ≤ 32` and most pixels not hitting a bubble, the loop terminates early via `break`.

Pseudo:
```glsl
vec3 col = texture(iChannel0, rd).rgb;
float t = 0.0;
for (int iter = 0; iter < 32; iter++) {
    if (iter >= uCount) break;
    int pick = -1;
    float bestT = 1e9;
    for (int j = 0; j < 32; j++) {
        if (j >= uCount) break;
        if (used[j] != 0) continue;
        vec2 h = ray_sphere(ro + rd*t, rd, Bubbles[j].xyz, Bubbles[j].w);
        if (h.x > 0.0 && h.x < bestT) { bestT = h.x; pick = j; }
    }
    if (pick < 0) break;
    used[pick] = 1;
    // shade bubble[pick] using ro' = ro + rd*(t+bestT), composite onto col
    // (front-to-back alpha blend, alpha = 1 - transmit*Fresnel_inverse ≈ 1 - exp(-d_wall))
    float alpha = clamp(1.0 - exp(-uAbsorbAvg * bestGlassChord), 0.0, 0.95);
    col = mix(col, shaded, alpha);
    t += bestT + 0.01;
}
```

`uCount` is the runtime number of bubbles. `used[]` is a fixed `int[32]` initialized to 0 once per `mainImage`. The inner `for j` uses dynamic indexing into `Bubbles[j]` — **this is the one risky pattern**: the v0.2 plan flagged it. Mitigate by: (a) capping `uCount` at 32, (b) declaring `Bubbles` as `uniform vec4 Bubbles[32]` so it's a uniform array (uniform arrays allow dynamic indexing in GLSL ES 3.0; local arrays don't on some drivers). Set the uniform from JS each frame.

**This requires a small addition to the runner: a `uniform vec4 Bubbles[32]` and an `int uCount` driven by the slider.** Detailed below.

### Camera

Same as v0.2: iMouse-driven orbital cam, 60° FOV, identical 4 base sines (extended to 32 via per-index phase). No change.

### Constants / uniforms

```glsl
const float IOR       = 1.50;        // crown glass
const vec3  ABSORB    = vec3(0.20, 0.05, 0.02);  // amber tint, weak
const float F0        = 0.04;
const int   N_MAX     = 32;

uniform int   uCount;        // 1..32, default 8
uniform float uWallThickness; // 0.02..0.85, in units of R_out
uniform vec4  Bubbles[N_MAX]; // xyz = center, w = R_out
```

`uWallThickness` is the single user-controlled knob. `uCount` is a second slider so the user can A/B between "few big bubbles" and "many small ones".

## Runner changes (small, scoped)

`js/runner.js` does not currently have a slider. Add a minimal pipeline:

1. **`buildFSHeader`**: when shader metadata declares `uniforms: ["Bubbles", "uCount", "uWallThickness"]`, append their declarations to the header. Otherwise, no change. (The current header is hard-coded; this needs a small patch — see step 3.)

2. **`getUniformLocs`**: extend the names list to include `Bubbles`, `uCount`, `uWallThickness`. Return null when absent.

3. **`frame`**: after standard uniforms, look up `Bubbles`/`uCount`/`uWallThickness` locs and upload. `Bubbles` values come from a small JS-side state object (`window.__appState.bubbles`).

4. **`ui.js`**: append two `<input type="range">` to the `#bar` near the shader buttons. On `input` event, update the state object. Recompute animated positions on each `requestAnimationFrame` *only if the current shader uses Bubbles* (so old shaders don't pay any cost). Store the animated centers in a preallocated `Float32Array(32*4)`.

5. **`index.html`**: add a `<div id="bubble-controls">` with the two sliders, hidden by default. `ui.js` shows/hides it when a shader is selected that declares `Bubbles` in its metadata.

The animated center formula matches v0.2: 4 base sins + per-index phase. Reuse a single function `bubbleCenters(time, count, out)` on the JS side.

window.SHADER_XbXXzB_hollow = {

  "title": "hollow glass bubbles (slider)",

The runner looks for `meta.uniforms`; if present, appends the declarations to the FS header (after `iChannel*` lines) and resolves their locations. The shader source itself can use the uniforms without redeclaring them, since they come from the header.

## Cost & risk

- **Best case** (sphere SDF + analytic ray-sphere intersection, no inner march): ~`uCount` ray-sphere calls + a fixed amount of math per hit. At 32 bubbles, ~32 ray-sphere per pixel. Cheap.
- **Worst case** (`uCount = 32` × `32` iter loop = 1024 ray-sphere calls per pixel): noticeable. Mitigation: the inner `j` loop `break`s once `iter >= uCount` AND once all front-most bubbles have been drawn (no remaining valid hits). Most pixels exit after 0–4 iterations.
- **Wall-thickness slider degenerate**: at `uWallThickness > R_out`, inner radius becomes negative. Clamp in JS: `uWallThickness = min(value, 0.85)`. GLSL also clamps: `R_in = max(R_out - uWallThickness * R_out, 0.001)`.
- **Total internal reflection on entry**: when the IOR/refraction direction is degenerate, fall back to pure reflection (already in the plan).
- **No reflection bounces**: v0.2's 2-bounce chain is dropped for v0.3 to keep the cost bounded at 32 bubbles. The visible-wall Fresnel from the outer surface already gives the rim highlight. Reflection bounces can be re-added in v0.4 if the look is too "single-bounce".

## Out of scope

- True volumetric march inside the wall (use the chord approximation; it is correct for a uniform-density spherical shell at this scale).
- Reflection bounces (1 bounce only — the outer-surface reflect()).
- Per-bubble IOR variation (one shared IOR; v0.4 can split per-bubble).
- Dispersion (cheap fake via mixing two env samples; not chromatic).
- Editing the 5 existing shaders.

## Why this is simpler than v0.2's A1/A2/A3 triplet

- One file, one algorithm, no SDF raymarch, no chain refraction, no per-bounce budget.
- Slider replaces the A1 vs A2 vs A3 trade-off the user was choosing between: the user now tunes **wall thickness** and **bubble count** instead of picking a whole different renderer.
- Reflection + refraction from **both** surfaces is the design goal, not an after-thought — it's the central feature.
