# Plan 0.4.4 — heightmap scene: a piece of glass landscape

From `0.4.3-object-shape-mesh-draft.md`, second half: a new scene where a
simplex heightmap is constrained to the **top face of a block**, plus a
**lake**, so the result reads as a piece of landscape made of glass. The draft
asks for two looks:

* **A — all glass.** Heightmap block drawn as glass, lake in blue-tinted glass.
  A transparent landscape floating in the sky.
* **B — opaque land, glass water.** Heightmap block in a solid height-colourised
  material (sand → grass → rock → snow), lake drawn as glass water. A landscape
  you can read topographically, with the water doing the refracting.

Both are one scene with a style switch, not two scenes: *where* the objects are
and *how* they move is identical, only the land's material differs.

## Model — scene `terrain`, `uScene == 4`

| | |
|---|---|
| id / label | `terrain` / `glass land` |
| `uScene` | 4 |
| native shader | `analytic_layers` (the renderer that already composites world geometry at its measured depth) |
| environment | the existing pastel sky |
| motion | objects drift in a band above the ridge line, inside the block's footprint |
| world geometry | land block with a simplex-displaced top face + lake, both finite |
| framing | `camDist` 16, `camPitch` -0.30 (from above; the orbit's pitch is positive when looking up) |

Adding the value 4 is inert until the land block opts in, which is what makes
this scene cheap: every scene-gated branch in the codebase compares
`uScene == 3` (`js/scene.js` feeds, `js/camera.js` cage picking,
`GLSL.cageOverlay`, `analytic_layers`' wire pass), and `GLSL.env`'s dispatch ends
in `if (uScene >= 3) return envPastel (d);` — so `terrain` inherits the pastel
sky with no change, which is exactly right: the land block *is* the ground, so
the scene must not also draw `envChecker`'s infinite plane at `y = -3`.

A new `GLSL.land` block mirrors the `GLSL.cageOverlay` precedent: the shared
scene owns the world-space geometry, the material renderer keeps drawing its own
objects, and the block is a no-op unless `uScene == 4`.

## Geometry

### Land — a block with a displaced top

```glsl
float landHeight (vec2 xz);   // uLandBase + uLandAmp * fbm3 ((xz + offset) * uLandFreq, drift)
```

The solid is the block `|x|, |z| ≤ uLandSize`, `y ∈ [bottom, landHeight]`, with
`bottom = uLandBase - uLandAmp - LAND_SKIRT` (`LAND_SKIRT = 1.0`): one unit of
flat base under the deepest possible valley, so the sides and the bottom are
flat faces and the landscape reads as a finite slab of terrain rather than an
infinite plane or a ten-unit-deep cube. Its bounding box is
`y ∈ [bottom, max (uLandBase + uLandAmp, uLakeLevel)]`, so rays above the ridge
line never march.

### Lake — the water column, not a water sheet

The water is the region `landHeight < y < uLakeLevel` inside the footprint. A
ray that enters the lake keeps travelling until it reaches the submerged
terrain, so the chord length drives Beer-Lambert absorption: the shallows stay
pale while the deeps go blue-black. A thin slab cannot do depth, and its
underside would z-fight with the terrain it is supposed to cover.

Land and water together have one top surface, `S (xz) = max (landHeight, uLakeLevel)`,
and one march finds it; the material at the crossing is water where
`landHeight < uLakeLevel` (flat normal `+y`) and land otherwise (normal from the
heightfield gradient). A ray entering the block through a side or the bottom
below `S` hits that face directly: land if below `landHeight` there, water
otherwise, normal = the face normal.

### Marching a heightfield — fixed steps in `t`, then bisection

```glsl
// first crossing of p.y < S (p.xz) on [t0, t1]: uLandSteps equal steps, the
// crossing interval refined by 5 bisections. -1 when nothing is crossed.
float landMarch (vec3 ro, vec3 rd, float t0, float t1);
```

The obvious alternative — sphere-tracing `(p.y - h) * k` with a Lipschitz
factor `k` — was rejected after measuring the noise: the 3-octave fbm has
`max |∇fbm| ≈ 10.2` per unit frequency, so the factor would be
`1 / sqrt (1 + (uLandAmp * uLandFreq * 10.2)^2)`, i.e. ≈ 0.45 at the defaults
and 0.05 at the slider corner, and a sphere tracer with a small factor runs out
of steps on every grazing ray (a ray skimming a flat valley at height 0.1 needs
hundreds of steps) — visible as walls in the distance. Fixed steps along the
box span spend the whole budget where the ray actually is, cannot exhaust, and
cost exactly `uLandSteps` noise evaluations per land pixel; the bisection makes
the silhouette accurate to `span / (uLandSteps * 32)`. Reflection and refraction
rays never march the land (see "Cost"), so the sign-change argument against
`t`-stepping does not apply.

The only ray that starts inside the solid is a camera placed below the terrain;
that pixel is a hit at `t0` and the block fills the screen, which is the honest
answer.

### Simplex

`GLSL.simplex` — the Ashima 2D simplex gradient noise (`mod289` / `permute`
hashing, no texture, no library, no internet; AGENTS.md forbids all three) plus
`fbm3`: three octaves, rotated by `mat2 (0.8, 0.6, -0.6, 0.8)` and doubled in
frequency each, weights 1 / 0.5 / 0.25 normalised by 1.75, so `|fbm3| ≤ 1` by
construction (`|snoise| < 1`). That bound is what the motion feed relies on.
The existing `swirl()` is a sin-product wobble, not noise: too regular for
terrain silhouettes and it would band on the ridge line. The land is
time-independent by default; `uLandDrift` slides the octaves against each other
(alternating directions, so the relief morphs instead of scrolling) for a very
slow erosion-style drift, driven by `iTime` like the sky, not by the motion
clock.

## Material

The land is shaded by the block, not by each renderer, following `cageShadeTop`
(which already draws "generic opaque top balls" inside glass shaders rather than
asking five renderers for five hooks).

* **style 0 — glass land** (`uLandStyle == 0`): Fresnel-weighted `envSun`
  reflection of the sky, refraction into the block, the refracted ray carried
  analytically to the block's exit face (`boxHit` from inside), refracted out
  through that flat face and looked up in `env`, Beer-Lambert over the chord
  with a faint green-cyan tint, plus a small sun-facing volume scatter term —
  under the near-uniform pastel sky the refraction alone carries almost no
  shading and the relief would not read. Water: the same model with the water
  IOR, `WATER_TINT` absorption over the water chord, and the submerged terrain
  (found by a second, half-budget march along the refracted ray) as what lies
  behind the water instead of the environment.
* **style 1 — colour land** (`uLandStyle == 1`): height- and slope-colourised
  opaque material — sand just above the lake level, grass, rock on slopes, snow
  near the top of the amplitude — with sun diffuse (`SUN_DIR`) and a small sky
  ambient. The lake stays the glass water of style 0, over the colourised
  submerged terrain. The lighting constants are deliberately dim: the shared
  ACES-style `tonemap` lifts midtones hard, and a physically "normal" sun
  intensity washes the land out to near white.

Objects are never seen *through* the glass land: they drift above the ridge
line, so they can only stand behind the block when the camera looks up from
below it, and the block then refracts the environment. `uLandStyle` is an
`options` param gated with `"scenes": ["terrain"]`, so it renders as a
`<select>` in the existing parameter strip and disappears in every other scene
— no new UI machinery.

## Depth composition with the objects

The land is world geometry that can be in front of or behind the drifting
objects, so it must be composited at its measured depth — not painted over the
final colour. The block therefore takes the shader's nearest object depth:

```glsl
vec3 landOverlay (vec3 behind, vec3 ro, vec3 rd, float objT);   // objT = BIG if nothing was hit
```

Land nearer than `objT` → the land wins the pixel; otherwise the objects stay
on top. Every renderer already computes a first-hit `t` internally (`h0.t` in
`analytic_layers`, the first march result in `thick_raymarch` / `thick_chain`,
the first membrane event in `hollow_bubbles`, the nearest opaque layer in
`thick_analytic`), so passing it costs one variable. The wire cube stays
cage-only; the two overlays are mutually exclusive by scene. The overlay is
applied per AA sample where a shader supersamples (inside `trace()`), so the
ridge line is antialiased like everything else.

## Motion — objects above the ridge line

The scene owns motion, so `js/scene.js` gains a terrain branch in the existing
scene-aware feeds (`bubbles`, `bubbles61`, `sceneBubbles4`):

```js
// after the regular motion wrote the buffer: x/z scaled into the footprint,
// y remapped into the band [ridge + radius + gap, ridge + radius + gap + LAND_BAND]
function liftAboveLand (out, n, meta, extentXZ, extentY) { ... }
```

**Simplex is not reimplemented in JS.** Exact per-object ground clearance would
need the same noise on both sides — duplication, and a drift hazard. Instead the
feed uses the conservative bound the sliders already give: the ridge line is at
most `uLandBase + uLandAmp` (because `|fbm3| ≤ 1`), so every object's bottom
stays above it. Same trick as the cage's `cage - radius` bound. The motion
extents (`extentXZ`, `extentY`) are computed once per cloud in `makeCloud` from
its orbit radii and drift amplitudes, so the remap is an affine map of the
existing motion, not a clamp. `uSpread` is hidden in this scene (it already is
in the cage); `uSize` keeps working; `uTopCount` / `uGravity` stay cage-only.

Picking is untouched: the objects come from the same feeds, and the land is
deliberately not pickable — clicking it misses, which already deselects.

## Camera

The land block (`uLandSize` 3…10) is much bigger than the cage (2…4.5), so the
default `dist = 7.5` starts inside the landscape. Every shared scene entry now
carries its framing:

```js
{ id: 'terrain', label: 'glass land', value: 4, native: 'analytic_layers',
  camDist: 16.0, camPitch: -0.30, hint: '...' }
```

`Cam.frame (entry)` applies it when the **scene changes** (`ui.pickScene`),
never on a shader change, so a shader switch keeps the user's view while a scene
switch presents each scene from its own default. The framed pitch also becomes
the pitch the `auto` camera mode settles on (`Cam.autoPitch`), otherwise auto
mode would drift back to the cloud's slight upward look and show the land block
from underneath. The other shared scenes carry the current defaults
(7.5 / 0.15), so coming back from the terrain does not leave the cloud tiny at
distance 16. `MIN_DIST` / `MAX_DIST` (2.2…40) already cover it, and the 0.4.2
viewport-relative grab drag needs no adjustment: a bigger subject is framed by
zooming out, not by a different turn rate.

## Parameters (all `"scenes": ["terrain"]`)

| param | type | range | default | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `uLandSize` | float | 3.0 … 10.0 | 5.0 | block half-size = the footprint of the landscape |
| `uLandAmp` | float | 0.2 … 3.0 | 1.1 | ridge amplitude around the base height |
| `uLandBase` | float | -2.0 … 2.0 | 0.0 | height of the mid plane the relief sits on |
| `uLandFreq` | float | 0.05 … 0.6 | 0.18 | simplex frequency (feature size) |
| `uLakeLevel` | float | -1.5 … 1.5 | 0.15 | water plane height; valleys below it fill |
| `uLandStyle` | int, options | glass / colour | glass | look A or look B |
| `uLandSteps` | int, options | 32 / 64 / 96 | 64 | march budget for the land |
| `uLandDrift` | float | 0.0 … 0.3 | 0.0 | slow drift of the heightfield |

The eight entries are declared once, as `GLSL.landParams ()` next to the block
(the same file owns the uniforms the block reads), and each supporting shader
spreads a fresh copy into its `params` — values stay per shader like every other
param. `uScene` stays hidden and owned by the scene row, exactly as today.

## Cost, and how it is kept down

The land march is the most expensive world geometry in the project:
`uLandSteps` × 3-octave simplex per ray, on every primary ray, in addition to
whatever the material renderer does.

1. **Analytic bounding-box pre-test.** One ray/box test against the land block's
   bounding box (top = ridge line, not the whole cube) rejects the sky and most
   of the frame before a single noise evaluation, and the shader's own nearest
   object depth rejects pixels the objects cover.
2. **Primary rays only.** Reflection and refraction rays inside the material
   renderers sample `envSun` for the land instead of re-marching it (the same
   simplification `cageOverlay` makes for the wire cube). Multi-bounce renderers
   therefore pay the land once. Inside the block itself, only the water pays a
   second march (for the submerged terrain).
3. **`uLandSteps`** for integrated GPUs, defaulting to 64; the global `aa`
   button already caps SMAA at 2K and the shader-level `uAA` stays opt-in.
4. The block is compiled only into the five scene-aware shaders, and only
   reaches the noise when `uScene == 4`.

## Validation

Dev-only tooling in `tools_GPU/` (gitignored; see plan 0.4.3 for the toolchain,
including the bundled Chromium that renders WebGL2 through SwiftShader).

* **`check-glsl.mjs`** — glslang type-check of the assembled fragment source for
  all five renderers (the scene and the style are uniforms, so one compile
  covers all six scenes and both looks).
* **`check-land.mjs`** (node, no browser): the JS side of the contract —
  * the terrain branch of every scene-aware feed is finite, inside the
    footprint (`|x|, |z| ≤ uLandSize`) and has every object's bottom above the
    ridge bound (`y - w ≥ uLandBase + uLandAmp`) for every index, time and
    slider corner, and the objects stay in a band of bounded height;
  * a JS reference implementation of `fbm3` (same constants) has
    `|fbm3| ≤ 1` over 10⁶ samples — the bound the motion feed relies on;
  * `Cam.frame` applies `camDist` / `camPitch` and leaves an entry without them
    alone.
* **`check-ui.mjs`** (real `index.html` in the bundled Chromium): 11 shaders × 6
  scenes select without error; `terrain` is `alt` for the six own-camera
  shaders and switching to it moves the shader to `analytic_layers` with a note;
  the land sliders appear only in `terrain` and `uLandStyle` renders as a
  `<select>`; picking the scene applies `camDist` / `camPitch` and a shader
  switch afterwards leaves the camera alone.
* **`render-check.mjs`** (same Chromium): renders the five renderers in the
  terrain scene in both styles at a small size, asserts a non-blank, non-NaN
  frame, and writes screenshots: look A reads as a transparent glass landscape
  with visible submerged terrain through the lake; look B reads topographically
  with glass water; objects sit above the terrain; no marching stair-step on the
  ridge line.

## Out of scope

* heightmap assets, textures, real DEM data — the terrain is procedural;
* water simulation, waves, gerstner normals, foam, caustics and screen-space
  reflections (a `uWaterRipple` normal perturbation is the most that may be
  added later);
* an infinite or chunked/LOD terrain — the point is a finite piece of landscape;
* terrain collision or resting contact for the objects (they float above it);
* seeing the objects through the glass block (the block refracts the environment);
* picking the land, and any land-driven camera framing beyond `camDist` /
  `camPitch`;
* teaching the own-camera shaders and the Shadertoy originals this scene — the
  same "difficult to implement, so skip" rule as everywhere else in the project.
