# v0.4 — hollow glass bubbles, real membrane wall (implementation plan)

Derived from the brief in `0.4-plan-hollow-glass-bubble.md`:
*"up to 32 hollow glass bubbles, specific thickness of glass walls (GUI slider), fast,
reflection and refraction from both surfaces, smooth (not noisy), correct z-ordering and
intersected spheres, looks good > physically exact."*

v0.2 stays as the fast/simplified reference (solid marbles). v0.4 is the full-feature one.

## Model: every wall is an event

v0.3 proposed "shell + cavity + shell resolved inside one bubble, then move on". That breaks
on the two cases the brief explicitly asks for — **intersecting spheres** and **z-ordering** —
because a bubble is consumed as one atomic unit, so anything sitting inside or overlapping it
is skipped.

v0.4 uses a different decomposition. A ray does not hit *bubbles*, it crosses **membranes**.
The renderer walks the ray forward and, at each step, handles the single nearest wall
crossing among all active bubbles:

```
col = 0 ; tp = vec3(1)                       // throughput
repeat uLayers times:
    find nearest sphere crossing ahead (t > eps) over uCount bubbles
        entering  when t == t_enter   (we are outside that bubble)
        exiting   when t == t_exit    (we are inside it, e.g. nested/overlapping)
    no crossing -> break
    crossWall(): refract in at surface A, traverse the glass, refract out at surface B
                 -> adds Fresnel reflections of A and B to col, multiplies tp,
                    leaves the bent ray in (ro, rd)
    tp small -> break
col += tp * env(rd)
```

Properties that fall out of this for free:

* **z-order is exact** — events are consumed in increasing `t`, and each contributes through
  the accumulated throughput of everything already crossed.
* **intersecting / nested bubbles just work** — a bubble whose wall lies inside another one is
  simply the next event; the "exiting" case covers a ray that starts inside a sphere (camera
  inside a bubble, or exiting bubble A while already inside bubble B).
* **both surfaces of every bubble are visible** — the near wall and the far wall are two
  separate events, so you see the far side of the membrane through the near side. That is the
  visual signature of a *hollow* bubble; a solid marble cannot produce it.

### What one wall crossing does

Given the outer radius `R` and inner radius `Ri = R * (1 - uWall)`:

| entering | exiting |
|---|---|
| A = outer sphere at `t_enter`, facing normal `+radial` | A = inner sphere at its far root (fallback: outer), facing normal `-radial` |
| glass chord = analytic distance A → inner sphere (fallback: back to the outer sphere when the ray misses the cavity — the thick lens rim) | glass chord = analytic distance A → outer sphere |
| B = the sphere the chord ended on | B = outer sphere |

At A: Schlick Fresnel `F_A` → `col += tp * F_A * (env(reflect) + sun) * filmTint`, `tp *= 1 - F_A`,
then `rd1 = refract(rd, nA, 1/ior)` (TIR → mirror the ray and stop the transmission).
Across the glass: `tp *= exp(-absorb_i * uDensity * chord)` (Beer-Lambert, so the rim — where
the chord is long — is visibly denser than the middle, which is what makes a wall read as a wall).
At B: Fresnel `F_B` reflection added the same way, `rd2 = refract(rd1, nB, ior)` (TIR → reflect).
Continue from B with `rd2`, offset by `eps`.

Because A and B are the two faces of the *same* wall, a thin wall barely deviates the ray
(bubbles are transparent, soap-like) while a thick wall behaves like a meniscus lens
(marble-like). One slider walks continuously between those two looks — exactly the knob the
brief asks for.

### Look, on top of the geometry

* **thin-film iridescence** — the reflected component is tinted by a cosine palette driven by
  `uWall * R * cos(theta)`, i.e. optical thickness. Smooth, analytic, no noise. `uIrid` slider.
* **dispersion** — the exit direction is split into three slightly different refracted
  directions (`ior * (1 ± uDisp * 0.02)`) and the env is sampled per channel. Smooth colour
  fringes on the rim, no sampling noise. `uDisp` slider.
* **per-bubble tint** — hue from the bubble index (golden-ratio palette), so overlaps read.
* **sun** — one analytic specular lobe in the env lookup so the membranes get highlights.
* **tonemap + gamma** — ACES-ish curve; prevents the stacked reflections from clipping.

## Cost

Search = `uLayers × uCount` ray/sphere tests (≈ 6 × 14 = 84 quadratics/px at defaults, 192 at
the 32-bubble max), plus ≈ 6 events × (2 refracts + 2 Fresnels + 4 cube samples). No marching,
no SDF, no stochastic sampling. This is *cheaper* than v0.2's raymarched variants while doing
strictly more.
Early-outs: `break` on no crossing and on `max(tp) < 0.02`.

## Parameters (auto-generated GUI)

| uniform | range | default | what |
|---|---|---|---|
| `uCount` | 1 – 32 | 14 | active bubbles |
| `uWall` | 0.005 – 0.5 | 0.06 | wall thickness as a fraction of the radius — **the brief's slider** |
| `uIor` | 1.0 – 2.0 | 1.45 | glass IOR |
| `uDensity` | 0 – 3 | 0.7 | Beer-Lambert strength |
| `uIrid` | 0 – 1 | 0.55 | thin-film iridescence |
| `uDisp` | 0 – 1 | 0.35 | dispersion |
| `uLayers` | 1 – 10 | 6 | max wall crossings per ray |
| `uSpread` | 0.5 – 2 | 1.0 | scene scale |
| `uAA` | 0 / 1 | 0 | 2×2 supersampling |

## Plumbing (the runner has no uniforms/GUI today)

Generic, opt-in, and inert for every existing shader:

1. **`js/runner.js`**
   * `buildUniformDecls(meta)` emits declarations for `meta.params` (scalars) and
     `meta.arrays` (e.g. `uniform vec4 uBubbles[32];`), inserted between the header and the
     shader source. Shaders never redeclare them.
   * `select()` resolves the extra locations and allocates one `Float32Array` per array
     uniform (once, not per frame).
   * `frame()` uploads scalars from `param.value` and arrays from a **feed** function, both
     allocation-free.
2. **`js/scene.js`** — `window.Feeds.bubbles(time, meta, out)` fills the `vec4(centre, radius)`
   array with 32 deterministic drifting orbits (golden-angle placement, per-index speeds, no
   two in lockstep), returns the active count. Pure math into a preallocated buffer.
3. **`js/ui.js`** — builds the slider strip from `meta.params` on shader select, hides it for
   shaders without params, live readout per slider.
4. **`index.html`** — one `<div id="params">` row + two script tags.
5. **`shaders/hollow_bubbles.js`** — the shader above.

## Validation

* `node --check` on every touched JS file.
* `node tools_GPU/check-glsl.mjs` — real ESSL 3.00 compile of the exact assembled source
  (now also emitting the param/array declarations), for all shaders.
* `node tools_GPU/cpu-preview.mjs` — a CPU port of the same wall-crossing math that renders a
  PNG offline, so the image can actually be *looked at* in a sandbox with no GPU. Used to check
  the wall reads as a wall at 0.02 / 0.15 / 0.45 thickness and that nothing goes black or NaN.
* Live preview in the browser (real GPU) with the FPS readout for the 32-bubble case.

## Deliberately not done

* True volumetric scattering inside the glass (chord-based Beer-Lambert is enough here).
* Inter-bubble refracted shadows / caustics.
* Merged soap-film walls between touching bubbles (that is the `smin` look of v0.2 B/C;
  hollow shells intentionally keep their own membranes so intersections stay legible).
